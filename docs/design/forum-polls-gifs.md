# Forums, polls and GIF favourites

Forum channels (posts, tags, pins, reactions, mentions), polls and their scheduler, and account-wide GIF favourites. Read before changing any of those routes or their client code.

Moved verbatim from the root AGENTS.md; keep it current when you change the behaviour it describes.

- A forum post or comment takes **any** file. It carries three lists — `image_urls`, `video_urls`, `file_urls` — capped together at `MAX_FORUM_MEDIA`. `forumAttachmentKind` (`lib/mediaTypes.ts`) decides which list a file joins by its type *and* its name, so only what a browser draws inline goes in the gallery (an `image/tiff` or `image/heic` would be a broken square); everything else is a download drawn by `FileAttachmentCard`, the same card the chat timeline uses
- A forum post belongs to **one forum channel** (`ForumPostRecord.channel_id`); listing, search and create take `channel_id` and check it is a forum channel of the room the caller can view. An empty `channel_id` is a room that is a forum itself with no forum channels. Posts from before that field are moved to the room's first forum channel at startup (`migrate_forum_post_channels`), and deleting a forum channel deletes its posts and comments. Anyone's post or comment may be deleted by someone holding `manage_forum_posts` in that channel (owners and moderators hold it; a custom role or a channel overwrite can grant it), checked against the post's channel
- Forum **tags** are set on the channel (`ChannelRecord.forum_tags`, `PUT /api/forum/{room_id}/channels/{channel_id}/tags`, sent whole) by its creator or anyone with `manage_channels`, and broadcast as an ordinary `m.channel.updated`. A post holds tag *ids*, checked against the channel's set on create/edit, so a rename reaches every post and a removed tag simply stops being drawn. A post's tags are edited by its author or by whoever may pin in that forum (its creator, `manage_channels` or `manage_forum_posts`) — a curator's edit may carry `tags` only, and is not marked as the author's edit (`edited` rides on the `forum.post.edited` broadcast for that reason). Forum **reactions** use the message reaction endpoint keyed by `post_id` — or `comment_id` for a reply — and `ForumReactions` reads `state.messageReactions` first so the `m.reaction` broadcast keeps every viewer live. A post's comments carry their reactions, fetched in one `$in` query (`get_reactions_for_events`); deleting a comment drops its reactions, since its tombstone shows nothing it said
- Forum **pins** (`POST`/`DELETE /api/forum/{room_id}/posts/{post_id}/pin`) are rows in `forum_pins` keyed by post id, not a field on the post, so the author editing their post cannot move one. The forum's creator (the channel's, or the room's for a room that is a forum itself) or anyone with `manage_channels` or `manage_forum_posts` may pin, up to `MAX_FORUM_PINS` per channel. A pinned post carries `pinned_by`/`pinned_at` and leads the **first** page of `list_posts`, newest pin first, and is excluded from the paged feed on every page — so the client pages from its last *unpinned* post (`orderForumPosts` keeps that order live). Deleting the post, its channel or its room deletes the pin
- Forum **mentions** are the chat ones: plain `@name` text, offered by `useTextareaMentions` (the forum composers are textareas, not contenteditable) from the same `findMentionMatches`, and drawn by `remarkMentions` inside `ForumMarkdown` — a remark plugin, so code spans and links are left alone. A forum post or reply notifies **only** the people it names: the connected half is `notifyForumMention` off the `forum.*.created` broadcast, the other half is a push with `mentions_only` set. Both broadcasts carry `suppress_role_mentions`, worked out the way `send_message` does it. An **edit** notifies only the names it *added* — `push::added_mentions` works them out once on the server, the push judges mentions against that (`mention_text`) rather than the body, and the `forum.*.edited` broadcast carries the same string as `added_mentions` so both halves agree. Clicking either half's notification opens the post: the push payload carries `post_id`, and `notification-navigate` (and the cold-start `?post=`) parks it with `requestForumPost`
- A **poll** is a message (`backend/routes/polls.rs`, `lib/polls.ts`, `PollCard.tsx`). The `m.poll` message carries what never changes — the question, the answers, `ends_at` — and `PollRecord`, keyed by that message's `event_id`, carries what moves. That split is the whole design: a poll needs no permission model, no listing, no deletion path and no place in search of its own, because it inherits every one of those from the message it arrived as. Redacting the message calls `purge_poll`, so deleting a poll is deleting its record and every vote in it. `POST /api/rooms/{id}/polls` runs exactly the checks `send_message` runs, and `m.poll`/`m.poll_results` are **refused** on the ordinary send path — sent from there they would be a card with no record behind it, unvotable and free to claim any numbers its author liked
- A vote is `PUT .../polls/{poll_id}/vote` and states the caller's **whole selection**, not a change to it: a retry casts the same vote rather than a second one, and an empty list is the only way to withdraw one. `poll_votes` holds a row per (poll, user, option) for the reason reactions are rows — a vote is written by one person and read by everyone. `total_voters` counts *people*, never the sum of the counts: one person ticking three boxes in a multi-select poll is one voter, and the bars are a share of that number, or they run past full
- Nothing on the request path decides when a poll ends — ending is the absence of an action, the same shape as going idle — so `run_poll_scheduler` does, every `POLL_TICK_SECS`. It claims a poll with an update filtered on `closed: false`, so the scheduler and an author pressing "End now" cannot both announce the same one, then reads the message back before announcing: a poll somebody deleted must not post its results into the channel an hour later. The results are their own message (`m.poll_results`) carrying the question, the answers, the numbers **and who voted for what** rather than pointing at the record — it has to read correctly in a search result years later, and the poll it came from can be gone. Two broadcasts, not one: `m.poll.closed` turns every open card final, and the results message puts them in the channel, which a client scrolled away from the bottom would otherwise not see
- Poll results are the one message with **no** notification on either side: the server sends no push for them and `maybeNotify` skips the msgtype. A clock posting to the whole room is not someone speaking to it, and the two halves have to agree or a person away from their desk is woken by the half that was not told. Each option on both cards opens a disclosure naming everyone who picked it — the whole list, never the first few, since that is the question it was opened to answer. Voting and opening it are two buttons on one row: a row that did both would make every look at who voted change the tally. A results message written before `voters` existed simply draws no chevron. Live state rides the message page (`get_polls_for_events`) the way reactions do — a channel of polls costs one request, not one per card — and reaches the client as `state.polls`, keyed by poll rather than held on the message, because one poll is on screen in the timeline, the pin list and a search result at once and a vote has to move all three
- Favourite GIFs and their **categories** belong to the account (`backend/routes/gif_favorites.rs`, one `gif_favorites` document per user) so they follow the user to every device. They change by **operation** (`POST /api/gif-favorites` with `op`: add/remove a favourite, create/rename/delete a category, file a GIF), never by replacing the document: favouriting happens on whichever device is to hand, and a whole-document save from one would drop what another just added. A save is checked against the `rev` it was read at and retried on a collision. The invariant lives in `apply_op` and is mirrored for optimistic updates in `applyGifFavoritesOp` (`hooks/useFavoriteGifs.ts`) — change both together: a category only ever holds favourites, so filing favourites, unfavouriting empties every category, and deleting a category keeps its GIFs. Category ids are minted by the client so a GIF can be filed into a category the same click created. Every change is pushed to the user's sockets as `gif_favorites` with `rev`; the client ignores one older than what it holds, and ignores all of them while its own requests are in flight, since the last answer is newer than any of them. Favourites kept in localStorage before this are merged in once by an `import` op and then removed
